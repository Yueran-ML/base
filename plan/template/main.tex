\chapter{Abstract}

The circular Fourier ring that appears in PCA of a grokked transformer's token embeddings is an iconic visualization in the grokking literature, and is often taken as evidence of the algorithm the network has acquired. This thesis shows that its timing runs the other way: the ring is a lagging indicator. By measuring three event times within the same runs of a 2-layer modular-arithmetic transformer under decoder-only weight decay, namely answer-aligned logit Fourier alignment $\taucirc$, behavioral generalization $\taug$, and embedding-Fourier consolidation $\tauf$ (each a BIC changepoint), we find $\taucirc < \taug < \tauf$, with the visible ring consolidating a median of approximately 6,000 steps after the network already generalises.

Three results establish that the embedding-ring lag is functional rather than cosmetic. First, time-resolved causal interventions locate the functional dependence: at checkpoints between $\taucirc$ and $\tauf$, removing the dominant answer-aligned Fourier subspace from the logits drops test accuracy by up to 0.72 while matched-energy random removals are negligible. Second, the three-event ordering holds in 48/54 runs and in 44/55 when re-trained on different GPU/CUDA hardware. Third, the lag holds in 89.8\% of 236 Grokking runs spanning two tasks, two primes, and a dense (lr, wd) grid, and a targeted slow-grokking sweep closes off the leading alternative explanation that the lag merely tracks generalization speed.

This sharpens prior observations that grokking coincides with the emergence of structure in the embeddings into a measured within-run lag, and complements circuit-formation accounts by isolating the visible ring as a distinct, later event.

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

Grokking, the abrupt onset of generalization long after training accuracy saturates, has become a canonical testbed for studying delayed representation learning in neural networks \citep{power2022grokking}. Subsequent work has extended grokking far beyond toy modular arithmetic \citep{liu2023omnigrok,miller2024grokking} and developed mechanistic and theoretical accounts of when and why the transition occurs \citep{nanda2023progress,varma2023explaining,lyu2024dichotomy,rubin2024grokking}.

On modular arithmetic tasks, \citet{nanda2023progress} showed that grokked transformers implement a Fourier circuit: the network uses trigonometric identities over a small set of task-relevant harmonics to compute modular addition. As a consequence, the $p$ token embeddings form a near-circular ring in PCA space. That visualization is compelling, but it conflates two questions: whether Fourier structure is causally used for the computation, and whether the embedding table itself has already settled into a Fourier geometry.

\section{Research question}

This thesis investigates the relative timing of three events during grokking:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\taucirc$: circuit onset, the BIC changepoint at which answer-aligned logit Fourier alignment rises above a permutation-null baseline;
  \item $\taug$: generalization onset, the first checkpoint where test accuracy $\geq 0.9$ sustained for three consecutive evaluations;
  \item $\tauf$: embedding-Fourier consolidation changepoint, the BIC changepoint at which token-embedding Fourier alignment rises above a permutation-null baseline.
\end{itemize}

The central question is: \emph{does embedding-level Fourier alignment consolidate before or after generalization?}

\section{Contributions}

This thesis makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{We localise where the Fourier computation is functionally load-bearing at the moment of generalization, and it is not the visible ring.} Time-resolved causal Fourier-subspace interventions show that removing the dominant logit-Fourier harmonics between $\taucirc$ and $\tauf$ drops test accuracy by 0.40--0.72, while matched-energy random removals are negligible (specificity ratios $12$--$214\times$).

  \item \textbf{A three-event timing framework makes the dissociation measurable within a single run.} Computing logit-level Fourier alignment alongside the embedding-level score at every checkpoint recovers $(\taucirc, \taug, \tauf)$ from one trajectory via BIC changepoint detection. The ordering $\taucirc < \taug < \tauf$ holds in 48/54 runs (88.9\%).

  \item \textbf{The embedding-ring lag holds across every regime we can reach.} Embedding-geometry consolidation lags generalization in 212/236 Grokking runs (89.8\%), spanning 1D weight-decay and learning-rate sweeps, a dense 2D grid, a different prime ($p{=}97$), and modular multiplication. A targeted slow-grokking sweep (24/24 G$<$F) closes off the leading alternative account that the lag merely tracks generalization speed.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Literature review}
\label{ch:litreview}

\section{Grokking and delayed generalization}

\citet{power2022grokking} introduced grokking on modular arithmetic. \citet{liu2022towards} constructed the (lr, wd) phase diagram identifying four training phases: Grokking, Memorization, Comprehension, and Confusion. \citet{liu2023omnigrok} showed that grokking extends beyond algorithmic tasks and is governed by the ratio of initial weight norm to the norm of the generalising solution. \citet{miller2024grokking} documented grokking outside neural networks entirely, suggesting the dynamics are an instance of a broader class.

On the theoretical side, \citet{rubin2024grokking} model grokking as a first-order phase transition, \citet{lyu2024dichotomy} prove that a dichotomy of early- and late-phase implicit biases can provably induce grokking in homogeneous networks, and \citet{mohamadi2024grok} give a margin-based theoretical analysis specific to modular addition. \citet{kumar2024lazy} reframe grokking as the transition from ``lazy'' to ``rich'' training dynamics. \citet{doshi2024grok} disentangle generalization from memorization on corrupted modular-arithmetic data.

\section{Mechanistic interpretability of grokked models}

The circuit-level view of neural networks \citep{olah2020zoom,elhage2021mathematical} treats trained models as collections of identifiable computational subgraphs. \citet{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered the Fourier circuit and showed via progress measures that circuit formation precedes the generalization jump. \citet{varma2023explaining} argue that grokking reflects the delayed ascent of an efficient generalising circuit over a more immediately learnable memorising solution. \citet{merrill2023tale} characterise this competition as a contest between sparse and dense subnetworks.

\citet{chughtai2023toy} study how networks learn group operations, finding that learned circuits depend on group structure. \citet{zhong2023clock} show that modular addition admits two distinct algorithms (``Clock'' and ``Pizza''), depending on initialisation and architecture. \citet{gromov2023grokking} provides analytic weight solutions for two-layer networks on modular arithmetic tasks.

\section{Embedding geometry and representation dynamics}

\citet{papyan2020prevalence} identified Neural Collapse, the terminal convergence of within-class activations to a simplex equiangular tight frame. Our $\tauf$ result is analogous in spirit: the Fourier ring geometry is a post-generalization consolidation rather than a prerequisite. \citet{barak2022hidden} demonstrated that abrupt generalization can hide gradual progress in loss-scale metrics, motivating our BIC changepoint approach.

\section{Implicit bias and weight decay}

\citet{soudry2018implicit} proved that gradient descent on exponentially-tailed losses converges to the max-margin solution on a much slower timescale than the loss decrease. \citet{galanti2024sgd} prove that SGD with weight decay biases learned representations toward low effective rank. The G$<$F lag we observe fits naturally with this picture: once the generalising circuit is in place, decoder-side weight decay continues to reshape the embedding rows through back-propagated gradients.

\section{Concurrent work}

Several recent papers study representation-level dynamics in modular-arithmetic grokking. \citet{khaliq2026scaffold} characterises a post-grokking ``scaffold'' phase. \citet{truong2026spectral} report that representation-covariance spectral entropy collapses before generalization. These works address complementary questions to ours.

\chapter{Background and theory}
\label{ch:background}

\section{Modular arithmetic and Fourier representations}

We study transformers on binary modular arithmetic: $(a+b) \bmod p$ for prime $p$. Following \citet{nanda2023progress}, the grokked network computes modular sums via trigonometric identities. Each token embedding $E_i$ for token $i$ ideally aligns with the angle $2\pi i / p$ on a circle in embedding space, producing the characteristic ``Fourier ring'' in PCA.

\section{Fourier alignment metrics}

Let $E_t \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times d}$ be the mean-row-centered token embedding matrix at step $t$. We compute the fraction of variance explained by the best single Fourier harmonic:
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{raw}}(t) = \max_{k=1}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}
  \left(1 - \frac{\|E_t - Q_k Q_k^\top E_t\|_F^2}
  {\|E_t\|_F^2}\right),
  \label{eq:f_raw}
\end{equation}
where $Q_k$ is an orthonormal basis for $[\cos(2\pi ki/p), \sin(2\pi ki/p)]_{i=0}^{p-1}$.

\section{Null correction}

With $p=53$ and $d=256$, any embedding matrix achieves non-trivial $F_{\mathrm{raw}}$ by chance. We estimate the 95th percentile under 100 random token-index permutations and define the null-corrected score:
\begin{equation}
  \fcorr(t) = \max(0,\, F_{\mathrm{raw}}(t) - F_{\mathrm{null,p95}}(t)).
\end{equation}

\section{BIC changepoint detection}

We apply a BIC-selected 1-break segmented regression \citep{schwarz1978estimating} to $\fcorr(t)$ on a log-time scale after exponential-moving-average smoothing ($\alpha=0.15$). A changepoint is accepted only if the 4-parameter model strictly improves BIC over the 2-parameter no-break baseline and the post-break slope exceeds 1\% of the total value range.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \fbox{\parbox{0.8\textwidth}{\centering\vspace{3cm}\textit{Illustration of BIC changepoint detection on a representative $\fcorr(t)$ trajectory.}\vspace{3cm}}}
  \caption{BIC changepoint detection on $\fcorr(t)$: the 1-break segmented regression (blue) vs.\ the no-break baseline (grey dashed). The vertical line marks the detected $\tauf$.}
  \label{fig:bic_example}
\end{figure}

\section{Logit Fourier alignment}

Analogously, $\flogit(t)$ measures the fraction of total logit variance explained by the best single Fourier harmonic of the answer class. Its BIC changepoint yields $\taucirc$, the circuit onset time.

\chapter{Experimental methodology}
\label{ch:methods}

\section{Task and model architecture}

We train a decoder-only transformer on $(a+b) \bmod 53$. The model uses $d_{\mathrm{model}}=256$, 4 attention heads, 2 layers, feed-forward dimension 1024, no dropout. All $p^2 = 2{,}809$ input pairs are generated; 30\% (843 pairs) form the training set. The embedding layer has fixed learning rate $\eta_{\mathrm{emb}} = 10^{-3}$ and weight decay 0; only decoder parameters are swept. We use AdamW with a 10-step linear warmup followed by constant learning rate, and gradient clipping at norm 1.0.

\section{Phase classification}

A run is classified as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Grokking}: both $\tau_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $\taug$ are detected, and $\taug - \tau_{\mathrm{train}} \geq 2{,}000$ steps.
  \item \textbf{Memorization}: $\tau_{\mathrm{train}}$ detected, $\taug$ not.
  \item \textbf{Comprehension}: both detected, gap $< 2{,}000$ steps.
  \item \textbf{Confusion}: neither threshold is reached.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental stages}

The experiments are organized into six stages:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stage 2: Weight decay sweep.} Fixed lr$=1.6 \times 10^{-3}$. Ten log-spaced wd values in $[1.2, 3.5]$. Three seeds. Total: 30 runs.
  \item \textbf{Stage 3: Learning rate sweep.} Fixed wd$=2.5$. Ten log-spaced lr values in $[5 \times 10^{-4}, 8 \times 10^{-3}]$. Three seeds. Total: 30 runs.
  \item \textbf{Stage 4: Circuit timing.} Retrain all Stage 2 and 3 cells with $\flogit$ logged every 500 steps. Total: 60 runs.
  \item \textbf{Stage 5A: Modular multiplication.} $(a \cdot b) \bmod 53$ with discrete-log reindexing. Same wd sweep as Stage 2. Total: 30 runs.
  \item \textbf{Stage 5B: Different prime.} $(a+b) \bmod 97$. Same wd sweep. Max 80,000 steps. Total: 30 runs.
  \item \textbf{Stage 6: Dense 2D grid.} $7 \times 7$ (lr $\times$ wd) grid, three seeds. Total: 147 runs.
\end{enumerate}

Training runs up to 50,000 steps, extended to 80,000 if train accuracy $> 0.9$ but $\taug$ not yet detected. All experiments use seed $\in \{42, 7, 2025\}$.

\section{G$<$F across weight decay (Stage 2)}

All 30 Stage 2 runs land in the Grokking phase. 29/30 runs (96.7\%, 95\% CI 83.3\%--99.4\%) show G$<$F ordering; the sole exception has $\Dtau=0$ (coincident). No Stage 2 run exhibits F$<$G ordering. The per-wd median $\Dtau$ fluctuates between 5,500 and 11,000 steps with no monotonic trend, confirming that G$<$F is a wide plateau rather than a localized peak.

Of the 30 Stage 3 runs, 25 land in the Grokking phase. Among these, 23/25 (92.0\%, CI 75.0\%--97.8\%) show G$<$F ordering. Augmenting the training loop with $\flogit$ yields the three-way ordering $\taucirc < \taug < \tauf$ in 48/54 Grokking runs (88.9\%, 95\% CI 77.8\%--94.8\%). Cross-hardware replication on different GPU/CUDA hardware yields 44/55 (80.0\%, inside the original CI).

\chapter{Causal interventions and robustness}
\label{ch:causal}

\section{Causal Fourier-subspace intervention (E7)}

At intermediate checkpoints between $\taucirc$ and $\tauf$, we remove the top-3 Fourier harmonics from the logit representation and measure the effect on test accuracy. The removal drops test accuracy by 0.40--0.72 across the eight addition-mod-53 cells that fully grok, while matched-energy random subspace removals drop accuracy by 0.000--0.013 (specificity ratios $12$--$214\times$).

A top-$K$ ($K \in \{1,3,5,10\}$) ablation spectrum and a frequency-selectivity control place the effect on high-power low-order harmonics specifically. An embedding-side intervention (I4) shows that Fourier content in the embedding is functionally necessary well before $\tauf$: at checkpoints between $\taug$ and $\tauf$, removing the top-3 Fourier directions from the token embedding reduces test accuracy from a baseline of 0.90--1.00 to 0.02--0.37. What consolidates late is therefore the visible global geometry, not the functional Fourier content.

Under discrete-log reindexing of multiplication, the same subspace carries no measurable load, which locates the boundary of the localization claim.

\section{Internal activation patching (pilot)}

A label-free, component-level activation-patching probe at six internal hook sites (embedding output, per-layer attention and MLP outputs, final residual stream) reproduces the Fourier specificity inside the network. At each site, we view each activation channel as a function on the $(a,b)$ input grid, ablate the top-$K=3$ grid-Fourier harmonics selected by activation power (no ground-truth label enters the construction), and compare against a dimension-matched random-subspace control (5 draws per site).

On the ten between-event ($\taug < t < \tauf$) addition-mod-53 checkpoints with base accuracy above 0.5, ablation at the final residual stream drops test accuracy by a median of 0.49 while the random control drops it by 0.02. The second-layer MLP output (\texttt{mlp\_L1}) shows drops of 0.66 vs.\ 0.10, and second-layer attention (\texttt{attn\_L1}) 0.77 vs.\ 0.21. On the $p=97$ cell the separation is sharper still (residual stream 0.44 vs.\ 0.00), and the multiplication cell replicates the E7 negative (ablation $\approx$ control at every site). The grid-space control is the informative comparison at the four sites whose activations mix both input tokens; at the embedding output and the first-layer MLP output, activations are (partially) separable functions of a single token, so the embedding side is read from the I4 intervention instead.

The probe localizes Fourier-specific functional necessity to the second-layer components and the final residual stream during the $\taug$--$\tauf$ window, adding an internal, label-free confirmation to the output-space interventions.

\section{Detector-specification sensitivity}

A 36-configuration sweep of the changepoint detector's hyperparameters (null interval $\in \{500, 2500, 5000\}$, EMA $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.15, 0.3, 1.0\}$, slope threshold $\in \{0.005, 0.01, 0.02\}$) yields mean robustness score 0.94. No G$<$F cell is reclassified as F$<$G under any configuration when $|\Dtau| \geq 3{,}000$ steps.

\section{Decoupled weight-decay ablation}

A $2 \times 2$ ablation (emb wd on/off $\times$ decoder wd on/off) shows that decoder weight decay is sufficient to produce both grokking and the G$<$F lag. A decoder-wd shutoff schedule confirms that continued weight decay after $\taug$ drives the remaining geometric consolidation.

\section{Ruling out speed dependence}

A targeted slow-grokking sweep (24 cells at low learning rates, $\taug$ up to 54,500 steps) yields 24/24 G$<$F, closing off the leading alternative explanation that the lag merely tracks generalization speed.

\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Embedding geometry as a lagging indicator}

The core finding is that the Fourier ring in a grokked transformer's embedding PCA is a post-generalization consolidation signal. The functional Fourier computation is already load-bearing in logit space well before the visible ring geometry has consolidated. This dissociation is measured within single training runs via three BIC changepoints, and confirmed by time-resolved causal interventions.

\section{Connection to Neural Collapse and representation dynamics}

The $\tauf$ result is analogous to Neural Collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} in spirit: both are post-generalization geometric consolidation phenomena. The difference is that the converged target is the cyclic-group representation rather than a simplex equiangular tight frame.

\section{Connection to implicit bias theory}

The G$<$F lag fits naturally with implicit-bias accounts of weight decay \citep{soudry2018implicit,galanti2024sgd}: once the generalising circuit is in place, decoder-side weight decay continues to reshape the embedding rows through back-propagated gradients, producing geometric reorganisation that postdates generalization.

\section{Practical implications}

Practitioners reading embedding geometry as a progress measure should expect it to understate how far the computation has already advanced. A clean ring means the algorithm arrived some thousands of steps ago, not that it is arriving now.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Task scope.} Results are established for modular addition and multiplication on $p \in \{53, 97\}$. Generalization to non-cyclic-group tasks, larger vocabularies, or non-algorithmic domains remains open.
  \item \textbf{Architecture scope.} All experiments use a 2-layer decoder-only transformer. A pilot at other depths found reversed ordering at 1 and 3 layers.
  \item \textbf{Measurement resolution.} All events are measured at 500-step granularity. Finer resolution could refine timing estimates.
  \item \textbf{Intervention controls.} Logit interventions (I1--I3) use a matched-energy random control (I3). The embedding intervention (I4) uses a different control design adapted to the embedding space.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Conclusion and future work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

The Fourier ring in a grokked transformer's embedding PCA is a lagging indicator of the algorithm it is taken to depict. By measuring three event times within the same runs, we recover the ordering $\taucirc < \taug < \tauf$ in 48/54 runs, with roughly equal spacing between consecutive events. The ordering holds in 89.8\% of 236 Grokking runs across two tasks, two primes, and a dense (lr, wd) grid. Time-resolved causal interventions confirm that the functional Fourier computation lives in logit space and is load-bearing well before the embedding ring has consolidated.

\section{Future work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Subtraction sweep.} Test whether the same timing pattern holds on $(a-b) \bmod p$, which uses the same Fourier basis but a different computational path.
  \item \textbf{Architecture-depth scan.} Systematically characterise how the ordering varies with transformer depth ($n_{\mathrm{layers}} \in \{1,2,3,4\}$), where preliminary results suggest the ordering reverses.
  \item \textbf{Online embedding intervention.} Intervene on the embedding during training (not just at evaluation) to test whether the late consolidation is functionally necessary for maintaining generalization.
  \item \textbf{Component-level activation patching.} Extend the pilot to a full $K$-spectrum and on-manifold control, providing component-level evidence for where Fourier structure is load-bearing inside the network.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

The practical consequence is a caution about a widely used diagnostic: embedding geometry read as a progress measure systematically understates how far the computation has advanced. A clean ring means the algorithm arrived some thousands of steps ago, not that it is arriving now.

\chapter{Experimental details}

\section{BIC changepoint estimator}

We apply an exponential moving average ($\alpha=0.15$) to the $\fcorr$ trajectory, then operate on $\log(1+t)$. We search all candidate breakpoints in the interior two-thirds of the sequence, fitting OLS regressions before and after each candidate. The 4-parameter breakpoint model is accepted if $\mathrm{BIC}_{\mathrm{break}} < \mathrm{BIC}_{\mathrm{no\text{-}break}}$, where $\mathrm{BIC}_k = n \log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + k \log(n)$.

\section{Null permutation estimation}

Each run maintains a fixed NumPy RNG seeded as $\mathrm{run\_seed} \oplus \texttt{0xDEAD}$, independent of the model RNG. We shuffle the $p$ token indices 100 times at every 500-step log point to estimate $F_{\mathrm{null,p95}}$.
